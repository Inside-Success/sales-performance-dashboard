Two substantive editorial boundaries remain:

1. **Buyer concern coverage:** in the NLCEO payment-plan case, the reviewer removed a four-payment request and explicit cash-flow explanation because the buyer did not say another schedule prevented purchase. It correctly avoided inventing resistance, but discarded a useful concern and the rep's accommodating response. Preserve material buyer concerns without requiring an ultimatum; distinguish them from routine information questions and do not manufacture weak handling.
2. **End-of-call commitment status:** some reports retain earlier promises after a clear decline. A decline does not automatically cancel an administrative promise, but historical intentions should not appear as live sales tasks. Judge each commitment against later evidence and distinguish outstanding administration from continued pursuit.

Some policy-boundary phrasing is still more audit-like than Tyler's preferred direct coaching. This is a smaller writing issue, not a new architecture defect.

Recommendation: retain this isolated implementation, but do not replace production with this exact candidate yet. The next change should address the shared reviewer concern threshold and final commitment status, with targeted positive/negative regression cases and the same representative corpus. Do not start another broad model tournament or rewrite the architecture. No fixed benchmark can prove every future report perfect; release judgment must be based on factual accuracy and usefulness, not just schema validity.
