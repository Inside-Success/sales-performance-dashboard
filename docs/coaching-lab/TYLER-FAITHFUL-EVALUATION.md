# Tyler-faithful coaching evaluation

Status: isolated experiment complete. Production remains unchanged. This is not a release approval.

## What changed

The first lab used an adaptation of the older coaching method plus Tyler's foundation reference. This phase instead starts from his actual latest judgment candidate, pinned to repository commit `5a892e080f0585ca0bdb662056651618c6f37fb5` and prompt SHA-256 `fc7d342c43f2d560fca2f2a17c6095a1e22a68b031e836f06ae947fa2ed3df08`.

Seven explicit text adaptations map the output contract, extend the reasoning to the three offer families, restore the documented unclassified-objection definition, and preserve evidence-based exclusion. Reversing those seven substitutions reproduces the original coaching/taxonomy prefix byte for byte. The old embedded offer snapshot is replaced with the governed source layers. The full foundation reference remains available to both writer and reviewer. The complete 33-file source snapshot and hashes are retained privately.

Mike's instruction is explicit: accept ACH/wire, but ask for the applicable down payment today to lock the buyer in. Do not merely wait for the transfer. The amount is offer-specific; the example is not universal. Credit the request separately from receipt, do not request another deposit where the first payment already cleared, and do not invent an effective date to penalize old calls.

## Evaluation design

- Six common real calls: two per offer family, identical full sources and candidate instructions for Terra, Luna, Haiku and Sonnet writers, each with Terra review. This tests writer allocation while holding the reviewer constant; it is not every possible combination.
- Compare against both prior-lab Terra/Terra and historical production reports. Neither is ground truth.
- Eight additional previously untested records: two main documentary, two reality and four Next Level CEO calls. The NLCEO records were selected by descending date from a read-only source query, excluding the earlier 40-source pool, before observing candidate outputs. Actual dialogue, not titles, determines eligibility.
- Nine known difficult cases are regression material, not a fresh holdout. Four existing synthetic ACH/wire boundaries are also regression probes, not real-call accuracy evidence.
- Anonymous comparison uses full transcripts and source-based findings. Model votes are advisory; specific claims must be adjudicated. Different reasonable lessons are not errors. No tuning against the final validation results while calling them untouched.
- The original $40 ledger remains authoritative; $15.49505050 was already settled before this phase. Every request is reserved before submission, settled from its usage receipt, and reused only by exact request identity. No replay of unresolved requests.

## Engineering boundaries

The experiment preserves the legacy production v2 contract. New isolated checks bind source, prompt, policy, writer receipt, reviewer receipt and exact reviewed content; a preview never represents delivery acceptance. Manual and official preview channels share the same accepted object. Prose repairs cover the new teaching fields but cannot change classifications, payment status, evidence, focus ownership or eligibility. A changed candidate requires fresh review.

Current production coaching and its safety/provider workflows remain unchanged. No Slack, dashboard or official/manual report publisher is connected to the lab. There is no local development server. The attended provider fixture has no schedule or production callback, and is deactivated after all paid requests are reconciled.

## Release interpretation

The goal is enough isolated evidence for a direct replacement decision, not an automatic recommendation for another live pilot. Passing tests, matching old outputs or winning model votes does not by itself prove usefulness, complete production integration or sales uplift. The final decision must state any demonstrated faults and any untested integration boundary specifically; it must not promise perfection or invent hypothetical defects.

The completed findings, boundaries and recommendation follow.

Haiku/Terra is the lower-cost alternative worth retaining, not silently substituting.

## Changes made in response to demonstrated faults

1. **Commitment ownership:** the original two-value next-step label could misrepresent a rep's explicit three-month follow-up promise as new advice. The isolated schema now distinguishes Agreed, Promised and Suggested. Material promised materials remain visible without repeating the coaching lesson.
2. **Reviewer output boundary:** one synthetic payment review returned a bare coaching report instead of its audit envelope. The validator held it. The review-stage adapter now explicitly states which envelope applies; a bare report is still rejected rather than treated as approval.
3. **Current versus historical policy:** uncertainty about Mike's earlier effective date must not suspend his current instruction. November 5 onward uses the current rule; earlier calls are not retrospectively penalized. The actual applicable first payment is requested today, without inventing a universal $4,500 deposit or claiming a pending transfer cleared.

The eight earlier fresh calls became regression cases after these revisions. Three further unseen calls were selected and fully read before their revised candidate outputs: one each from main documentary, reality and Next Level CEO. This is a small final validation sample, not a claim of company-wide error-free accuracy.

## Existing-format alternative

A separate three-call Terra/Terra arm retained Tyler's original production-shaped output schema, with only offer-family and taxonomy adaptations. It passed the existing draft/audit/render and unchanged-prose safety-boundary functions offline. It did not demonstrate better coaching: one report still overstated a reasonable seller proposal as pressure. Easier compatibility alone is not a reason to choose it. The safety-model decision and actual production delivery were not exercised by this arm.

## Integration consequence

The new report contract intentionally distinguishes promises from agreements, and alternatives from justified corrections. Those meanings cannot be honestly squeezed into the existing production enums without losing information. This branch therefore contains an isolated contract and explicit acceptance/repair adapters; it does not claim the existing live dashboard, report chat, Docs publisher or durable provider already consume the new schema. Production still uses its original contract.

A release must carry the selected report version through the existing manual and official workflow paths, durable checkpoint identity, reviewer and safety repair, Docs/dashboard/chat readers, generation labels and rollback. It must preserve old report reads, separate scoring/compliance and avoid duplicate delivery. That integration can be tested with isolated fixture/replay outputs before a direct controlled cutover; it does not require exposing sales reps to an unproven live pilot.


## Completed results

- This phase covered **26 distinct real calls** across all three offer families and four synthetic payment boundaries. Repeated outputs and model comparisons are not additional calls.
- The four-writer matched screen produced 24 reviewed reports. Initial validation produced 20 accepted reports and one correctly held malformed review. After the ownership/date/envelope revisions, all 21 regression/probe outputs validated, followed by three untouched real-call outputs.
- **68 source/receipt-bound report artifacts passed** the isolated acceptance and manual/official preview checks. A separate three-report native-format arm passed its existing-format checks. These are technical acceptance counts, not an accuracy percentage.
- All final revised reports were read against full-source observations. No material unsupported payment, eligibility, offer or commitment claim was found in the three final untouched outputs. They correctly handled a clear refusal, a recording ending mid-video and a buyer arranging payment with a Friday follow-up.
- **11 lab tests and 161 existing coaching/display tests passed**. The production build passed during this experiment. Receipt recollection left the settled total unchanged, with no unresolved paid request.
- One Sonnet transport gateway timeout was reconciled against its successful owned execution and original response. It was not replayed. One earlier bare-review response was held and retained as failure evidence; the revised stage returned the proper envelope on the retest.
- Total usage-priced experimental spend: **$22.035771735**, including **$15.49505050** from the preceding phase. This phase added **$6.540721235**. The ceiling was $40, not a spending target. These are receipt-based configured-rate estimates, not a provider invoice.

## Independent comparison and honest judgment

An anonymous Sonnet comparison preferred a new report on three of six common calls (Sonnet twice, Haiku once), and historical production on three. It never ranked Terra first. Those results are retained rather than hidden. Several of its purported factual findings did not survive transcript review: a rep's remark was incorrectly treated as buyer confirmation of email receipt; a later clarification question was confused with an earlier one; and successful payment was treated as proof that a prior retry answered the bank's status warning. Its rankings also sometimes rewarded optional extra coverage.

The appropriate conclusion is therefore narrower than either “the judge proved a winner” or “all new reports are better.” The new candidate is better calibrated about reasonable seller initiative, later recovery and historical rules. Its report has clearer ownership and less repetition. Production still sometimes supplies more specific teaching, while the new Terra report can be cautious and thin in selecting the most valuable lesson.

**Recommendation:** retain this Tyler-faithful foundation and the two-stage architecture. Terra/Terra is a sensible cost-conscious integration candidate; Sonnet/Terra can supply richer detail, but this screen does not establish a consistent quality gain worth its roughly threefold writer-plus-review cost. Keep Haiku/Terra as an alternative, with its greater dependence on review visible.

**Do not call this a ready-to-deploy replacement.** The reasons are concrete: the new schema is not yet wired through the existing production consumers/provider and safety path, and the evidence does not prove consistently better main-lesson selection. This is not a recommendation for an obligatory live pilot. The remaining work can be performed offline: preserve the strongest useful production lessons without restoring unsupported criticism, complete the versioned production adapters, and verify both full delivery paths against isolated sinks before proposing a direct cutover. No additional real-call cohort is required merely to accumulate a larger test count.
